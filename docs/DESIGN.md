# Design standard

MinMacs 2.0 has one face: the menu window, which opens from the one MinMacs icon. This page says how it looks, what it says and how it is built. The menus of before are still in the app and open when the pref `core.window` is off. Their rules are the second half of this page. A change that breaks a rule here is a bug.

## The menu window

One icon sits in the menu bar. A click opens a window under it, 1020 by 728 points, dark glass in every appearance. It never resizes. It closes as a menu does: on Escape, which first lets go of an active row, on a click outside, when another app comes to the front and when the space changes. Its first frame is drawn before it comes on screen.

The layout is prototype 195-a, Dock Below, with the dock made slim:

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2 agents ask you             (folded away when nobody asks)            |
+------------------+---------------------------+-------------------------+
| Servers          | Memory ~^~    Disk ___    | Worktrees               |
| Agents           | Today                     | Caches                  |
| Apps and helpers | Stopped :3000 Next.js.    |                         |
|                  | (or a pointed row's story)|                         |
+------------------+---------------------------+-------------------------+
| o 42.4 / 64 GB memory in use  ( Fix Everything 7 )  +3.1 GB memory  * |
|   182 GB disk free                                  7 fixes, x first   |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  the dock, 64 points: o is the ring, * the gear and the power icon
```

1. **Memory left, disk right.** What holds memory is on the left: servers, agents, apps and helpers. What holds disk is on the right: worktrees and caches. Memory and disk are two numbers everywhere and are never added into one.
2. **The middle.** At its top a strip shows memory in use and disk free, small and side by side: each its newest value, how far it moved and a small line. From the second minute each side is a real line over the minutes kept so far, and it says how long that is, "the last 4 min", until it spans "the last hour". The very first minute shows the newest value over an empty track, never a made-up line. Under the two sides two words, "hour · 7 days", choose what they show, the chosen one bright on a quiet pill, as a segmented control; they are real buttons, and the choice is remembered, a pref and no row in the log. On 7 days each side is one value an hour, the mean of the minutes MinMacs kept in that hour, which Core writes to the log's points off main as each hour ends, one point a side an hour, and keeps for 8 days, so it outlives a restart. The line runs from the first hour kept to the newest and says how long that is, "the last 5 h", "the last 2 days", until it spans "the last 7 days". An hour the Mac was off or asleep, or MinMacs was not running, is a gap where it was: no line is drawn across it, and an hour alone between two gaps is a dot. Until two hours are kept the side shows its newest value over an empty track and says it fills in as the days pass. The window reads the points only while it is open and on 7 days, off main, and again only when the hour turned or a new hour was written. The pointer on a side's line shows the value there and when, "12 min ago" or "Wed 07:00", or "nothing kept" in a gap, where the newest value and its move stand, with a hairline at its place; nothing is read for it. Under the strip is the log, down to the slim dock: what MinMacs did and decided, and each setting that changed, with who changed it. Each entry is one plain sentence, "Stopped :3000 Next.js. Undo starts it again.", with a quiet line under it: the time, who acted and the tokens a model call took. Undo is named only where the record can still be taken back, and there the quiet line ends in a small Undo press: a real button, which Tab, Space and VoiceOver reach. It runs the same restore as the notification's Undo, off main. While it runs the press is away and the line says "taking it back"; one that did not go through says why. A thing is one entry: an action taken back is told in its own entry with both times, "12:00, started again 12:05", and a Fix Everything run or a mode's pass is one line, "Fix Everything: 5 done, 2 skipped. 2 agents ask you.", with each thing it skipped and why in its tooltip and in what VoiceOver reads. Entries group by day, and the hourly reviews of a day that acted on nothing fold into one line where the newest of them stands, with the span they cover, "08:00 to 13:00", or one time when it is one minute. The log holds the newest 300 entries of the last 30 days, whole entries only, moved an entry at a time, with "12 more below" at its foot. On a new Mac it says what MinMacs watches and that it acts on nothing on its own in its first day, and never claims a result. The window reads the log only while it is open, off main, and again only when the log changed: no timer, and nothing on main but drawing. While the pointer rests on a row the middle shows that row's story instead, over all of it: its name and group, its number, and one line that says whether it asks, goes or stays and why, then its facts and where it stands in its group. A worktree's facts are its git in plain words, read from what WorkForest already knows: its branch, its last commit with subject, short hash and age, "3 ahead, 1 behind main", its uncommitted files, whether it was merged or its remote branch deleted, and who made it. Under its first line stands its pull request, when the person's own `gh` is there and logged in: "PR #142 · open · checks passing · review requested", the title, its milestone as a fact row, "Milestone   Phase 6: Bench and polish", when it has one, and an Open pull request press that opens the page in the default browser. The window asks gh only while that story shows, never on a tick, [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) "Pull requests"; while it asks, one quiet line says "Looking for a pull request", for at most 5 seconds, and an answer from before stands while a moved head is asked again. Draft, merged with when, closed, "2 checks failing" and "No pull request for this branch" read the same way. An agent's story says, under its line "Working. Last active 3 min ago.", what it has been doing, where it is and what it holds, each only where a fact holds it: its harness, its folder and branch, its session's title where the row's name is not that title, how long it has run, what it did in the last hour, "42 tool uses, 6 files edited in the last hour", from the part of its transcript Know already reads for the title, where it runs, "cmux, minmacs, agent story", with a small Go to press when MinMacs can bring that tab, pane or app forward, its worktree, "minmacs · agent-story", with that worktree's pull request where one is known, "#142 · open · milestone: Phase 6", the ports it serves and the servers in its process tree, its session's caches with their sizes, and the processes under it. A worktree, a server or a cache it names is a press in that row's group color that makes that row the active one, a member of a row that stands for several naming that row. Go to and these presses are real buttons, and only a person's press runs Go to. Its place in its group comes after them where room remains. A press on a row makes it the active row. The row under the pointer always shows its story, active row or not, while the active row stays blue in its list; once the pointer leaves the rows, onto the middle, a header, empty space or out of the window, the middle goes back, a short beat later, about a quarter second, to the active row's story, or to the log when no row is active. The pointer on the middle keeps whichever story shows, the row it came from or the active row, so that story's presses, Open pull request, Go to and its links, are reached by moving onto them. The dock's ring lights the arc of the row under the pointer, or else of the active row, and the numbers beside it are that row's.
   - **A row has three looks.** At rest it is drawn on the glass. Under the pointer it gets a quiet white wash, white at 7 %, with the row's rounded corners, and its text stays as at rest, so a calm name stays calm; in Increase Contrast the wash is 15 % with a thin white edge. Active, it is solid blue with white text, and VoiceOver reads it as selected. A press on a row makes it active and only selects: it never acts, and a row holds no press of its own, so nothing in it is taken from another press, such as the log's Undo. A press on another row moves the active row there. A second press on the active row, a press on empty space, a header or the dock, Escape, Settings, the window's close, or the row leaving the model, as when its process ended, lets it go; a press on the middle keeps it, since its story is what is read there. The arrow keys move the active row: up and down its side, group after group, left and right to the row of the other side at its height, and with none active to a side's first row, which scrolls into view; the middle then tells the active row's story until the pointer moves again. Tab and Space still go through the window's buttons. No look moves: no timer and no animation, by rule 12.
3. **The top line** says who asks the person, such as "2 agents ask you". When nobody asks it folds away and the lists get its height.
4. **The dock** is one slim band, 64 points high, at the window's foot: the lists and the middle get the rest. At its left a small ring shows memory by group, with the disk as the thin outer track, and beside it two quiet lines, "42.4 / 64 GB memory in use" and "182 GB disk free". Fix Everything stands in the middle of the window and runs every fix that does not need the person, in order. When there is something to fix it is the one vivid thing in the window: a warm orange pill that says "Fix Everything" with the count in a small capsule. Right of it one line says what a press gives back and one what runs first: "7 fixes, Wrap Up fix-auth-redirect first". While the pointer rests on the button it lifts and brightens, the gains brighten, and the numbers beside the ring are the Mac after the fixes. While a run plays, the button turns dark and fills with orange from the left as fixes land, and holds the step. "Fix 3 of 7" and a small Stop stand right of it with what came back so far, and a press on either stops the run after that step. As the run ends the button is bright again, with how it ended, a check when it did all it set out to, and what came back on its face; the end note stands right of it. With nothing to fix the button stays in place, calm, with a check and "No fixes to run", and takes no press. It says what a press would do, never that the Mac is clean: the window never claims a result.
5. **The orange mark** on a row says a press on Fix Everything acts on it. A row whose fix waits for its own press, such as a cache its tool cleans, carries none.
6. **The gains** stand right of the button on one line, a number for memory and a number for disk, "+3.1 GB memory · +18.6 GB disk", in their own inks. A side shows only when it is above zero, so a fix that gives no disk back shows no "+0 MB disk". While a run plays they count up, "so far". Once it ended, what came back stands on the button's face. With nothing to fix, what came back today stands there, "today +250 MB memory · +3.2 GB disk", by the same rule.
7. **Two buttons only:** the gear for Settings, also Command-comma, and the power icon for Quit, also Command-Q, small, at the right end of the dock. They stay in that place in Settings too, and Settings' cards end over them. There is no Hide Icon.
8. **No text is cut and none ends on an ellipsis.** A row's name first takes the room of a column with nothing to show in that row, then of the state word, then of the row's own hour, then of CPU. Each part of a row is drawn in the place worked out for it once, from the right, and what stood left of a part that gives way moves right into its place, so a name never reaches the part beside it. A name still too long wraps at a space, a name with no space between two characters, and the row grows by a line. Every name is measured with the real font, so wide letters fit too. Everywhere else a long text steps its type down, never under 10 points, and then wraps: the story's title, the dock's lines. The step in the running button takes two lines at most; a step still too long shows its action alone, "Moving to the Trash", with no name, since the count stands beside the button. The end note keeps whole sentences only, and where removed things went first: a Skipped list too long is shortened, "Skipped 12 agents that ask you and more.", before that sentence would go. The render checks read every drawn string on every made-up day, the welcome's too: no ellipsis, none past its box or its view, none over another.
9. **No row is cut in half.** Each group ends on a whole row. Where rows wait below, the last row shown fades as a whole and the header says "9 more below". A side with room to spare stays compact: its rows keep one line each, and the side closes right under them with what it holds in all: "This disk", free of the whole, and in use. What the rows hold is never summed, since one row can hold another. A side with no rows has those lines right under its notes.
10. **One rule for sizes.** Megabytes are whole. Gigabytes have one decimal, which stays when it is a zero, and a second one under 10 GB when it says more: "1.0 GB", "6.6 GB", "1.86 GB". From 100 GB up the number is whole, and from 1000 GB it is terabytes: "1.0 TB". Memory counts in 1024s. The disk, its free space and every size on the disk side count in thousands, as the Finder does, so a disk has one size everywhere.
11. **Quiet rows step back.** An idle server or agent, a helper, an app that is known to use next to no CPU, a cache that is known not to be in use or a worktree with nothing uncommitted has its name drawn a step dimmer, unless a fix touches it or it asks. A fact that is missing makes no row quiet.
12. **Few motions, none under Reduce Motion.** The glow around Fix Everything breathes once in 5 s while there is something to fix and the pointer is elsewhere. A press that starts a run sends one soft ring out from the button, and as the run ends one light runs across it while it settles from a touch larger. A row that is new gets a short light under it. The turn to Settings and back takes 0.3 s: what comes rises 6 points into its place. The breath, the ring, the light across the button and the row's light are layer animations: no timer runs for them. While a run plays, the button's fill and the gains follow the run's numbers, and a step's words rise in.
13. **Facts read as a person would say them.** Each fact is worded in one place, `MMWindowFact…` in the model. In a row's story it stands with its label, "Files" and "3 changed, 1 new". On a row's second line it is a phrase of its own, "3 files changed, 1 new", "2 commits not pushed", "untouched for 12 d", "in api", and only whole phrases stand there. How long has one form: "40 s", "12 min", "2 h 36 min", "12 d". Under ten seconds the words say "just now", never a zero. An agent's raw reason, such as "child caffeinate", is shown nowhere.
14. **A scrolled group keeps its place.** It keeps the row it showed first when a new model changes what is above, a model that comes while it is being scrolled leaves it alone, and at rest the rows that show share what is left at the group's foot, so no blank strip stands there.
15. **Worktrees come by project, the most active first.** A project is a worktree's repo. Worktrees are ordered by activity, the time of their last work, newest first, and a tie goes to the bigger, so the header says "by activity", where the other groups say "by memory" or "by disk". The story still says where a worktree stands by size, "The smallest of 18 worktrees". With one project the group is a flat list, its rows named as everywhere: "minmacs" for the main checkout and "minmacs · seam-130" for a linked worktree. With two or more, each project has a small sub-header in the group, its name and how many worktrees it has, in the quiet type of a header and a step lighter, with its worktrees under it. There a row needs only its branch, "seam-130", or says "main checkout", since the header names the repo; what VoiceOver reads names the project too. Projects stand in the order of their most active worktree. When the group has too little room, every project shows its most active worktree before any project shows a second, and before another group on the side shows more than its first row. The room left goes to the most active worktrees that wait, whichever project holds them. Under a project whose rows wait, one quiet line says how many, "3 more in barkbot"; where a line a project does not fit, one line at the group's foot says it once, "11 more in 5 projects". Those lines add up to what the header's "11 more below" counts. Each row stays whole and the last one shown fades, by rules 8 and 9. Scrolled, the group shows under them the rows that waited, each project under its sub-header again, and it keeps its place by rule 14; closed, it opens on the most active again.
16. **Rows that read alike stand as one.** Three rows or more of one kin are one row: the caches one registry row found, such as 169 families of empty temp folders, or helpers of one name that one worktree left behind. Two still read as two things; a third makes a list that repeats itself. The row is named in plain words from its registry row, "Temp folders left by tools", where the row's own title is a pattern, and its second line says the totals, "45,282 folders in 169 groups" or "4 sessions". Its size is the sum of the members that lie inside no other member, so nothing is counted twice: Claude Code's scratch holds each session's folder and, for a session that still runs, the entries of its scratchpad inside it, and the row counts the folder once with all it holds, and its count, its split and the members its story lists are those outer ones. Helpers whose process trees hold one another stay apart, and rule 9 still never sums what one row holds of another. Its quiet word is the one its members share, else "in use" when any is in use, else "kept": a member in use stops none of the others. Its story says after the lead how many can go and how many stay, "Goes with Fix Everything. 166 groups can go, 3 in use.", why some stay, and under the hairline its five biggest members with their own words and sizes, where another row shows its place in the group. It sorts by its size among the others: the header says "by disk", and the orange mark already says what Fix Everything takes, so that row does not jump to the top. Fix Everything still acts on each member as an action and a record of its own, so each comes back alone with Undo, and its gains are the members' own; the fix's title counts the row once, "Move 3 leftovers to the Trash", as the window shows it. Only the window and `minmacs window` show rows so. `minmacs window --json` gives such a row `collapsed`, with its count, how many lie inside another as `inside`, its kin and every member whole, and `minmacs clutter list` keeps every one apart.

### Settings in place

Settings is prototype 197-b. The gear turns the area under the top line into cards, in the same window, and back. Escape leaves Settings first. Opening MinMacs again and `minmacs://settings` bring MinMacs to the front and open the window turned over to Settings, or on the welcome until Start was pressed. A link that starts MinMacs waits for the launch.

Three cards that read at a glance, as System Settings does. Each card has its own color and mark, and a tinted wash and edge: **Keep this Mac awake** in amber with a cup, **Clean up on its own** in green with sparkles, **This Mac** in gray with a display. In each card a row is one line high, 44 points: a small tile in the card's color with the row's own mark, a short title and the switch. A row has a second line only when it says a state, such as "Awake now for 2 agents", "This Mac has no lid." or "1 ready. It waits for your press.". What a switch does is its tooltip and what VoiceOver says after its name, never a sentence under every row. The cards are as tall as their rows and stand from the top in two columns, each under the shorter column, so no card is stretched to fill the window: Keep this Mac awake with This Mac under it, and Clean up on its own beside them.

- **Keep this Mac awake** stands first, so it is never hunted for. Three switches: "Keep awake now" keeps the Mac from sleeping, as `caffeinate` does, until it is turned off (`insomnia.awake`); "While agents work" keeps it awake while an agent works and its quiet minutes after, and says for how many agents it does so now (`insomnia.agents`); "Lid closed" keeps it working with the lid shut (`insomnia.lid`), and says when it is paused, live or set by another app.
- **Clean up on its own**: one switch a rule, "Idle dev servers", "Apps when memory is low", "Finished worktrees" and "Build output and leftovers". The tooltip says what the rule does when on. On is the mode that acts on its own: Careful, or Eager for apps, where Careful acts on nothing. Off is Off. A rule set to Eager elsewhere shows on, with Eager's tooltip. Core keeps its three modes, and `minmacs mode` still sets any of them. While the rules that are on still only watch, in MinMacs's first day, the card's head has one badge, "Starts in 23 h", and no row says it.
- **This Mac**: "Open at login", "Pull requests", which lets a worktree's story ask the person's gh about its pull request (`workforest.pullRequests`), and one line at its foot, "Removed things go to the Trash.". There is no card and no choice for where removed things go: see "Where removed things go" below.
- When nobody asks, Settings starts at the window's top, as the menu does. On a Mac with no lid the lid switch is drawn as not available and takes no press.

A press applies at once. There is no Save.

What the Settings window of before held, and where it is with the menu window:

- **Keep Mac Awake, Awake While Agents Work and Keep Awake With Lid Closed** are the three switches of Keep this Mac awake: Keep awake now, While agents work and Lid closed.
- **The model reviews ask** has no place in the window. `minmacs review --model auto|haiku|sonnet|opus|luna` sets it.
- **Showing and hiding an icon, and One Icon,** mean nothing with one icon and one window, and are not there. The one icon is shown at every start, whatever was hidden before, since nothing in the window could bring it back.
- **An expert's own pane,** such as the keep-awake timings, is not in the window. Those prefs keep their values, and the Settings window of before still edits them when `core.window` is off.

### The welcome

The first time the window opens it shows the welcome alone, prototype 199-a: what MinMacs is in two sentences, the Open at login switch, three facts and Start. It asks no removal choice. Nothing is applied until Start, and Return presses Start. After Start the welcome never comes back.

On a Mac that runs unattended, decision D3, nobody would press Start, so there is no welcome. Unattended is the pref `core.unattended`, which `minmacs unattended on|off` sets, or `MINMACS_UNATTENDED=1` in the app's environment. At launch, and when `minmacs unattended on` reaches the running app, the welcome's defaults apply: Open at login is turned on and the welcome is marked done. Two setting rows by `unattended` say so, and the log reads "The welcome was skipped, since this Mac runs unattended. Its defaults apply." With a person at the Mac the welcome is as above.

- No login item is added before Start, unless the Mac runs unattended. For a person new to MinMacs the switch starts on. For a person who launched MinMacs before the welcome existed, it starts on what their login item is now.
- After Start the menu takes no press for a moment, counted from when it is really shown, so a second press where Start was runs no fix.
- Each fact is read from the code that makes it true, so it changes when the code does.

### Where removed things go

There is no choice to make. MinMacs never deletes anything at once: everything it removes keeps a way back. A merged worktree is removed by `git worktree remove`, so git can give its files back, and its ignored files go to a recovery folder first. Caches, build output, leftovers and untracked config files go to the Trash. A tool's own cleanup runs after an APFS recovery copy. MinMacs never empties the Trash. The welcome and Settings offer no removal choice, and `minmacs removal` only says this. The old pref `core.removal`, which once held Smart, Trash or Delete, is read by nothing that decides and is cleared at Core's start. [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) invariant 1 has the whole rule.

### Words in the window

- Plain names: Servers, Agents, Apps, Worktrees, Caches. The app is MinMacs. No shown string names LocoHost, WorkForest, Noo-Noo or Insomnia.
- Never claim a result, such as "all clean". Say what is there and what a press does: with nothing to fix the button says "No fixes to run", which is what a press would do.
- No exclamation marks, no emoji, no ellipsis, no asides in parentheses.
- "For good" and "permanently" are not used.

### How it is built

```
MMGraph snapshot --> MMPerformance --> MMWindowModelFrom --> MMWindowModel --> MMWindowPanel, MMWindowView
     any queue        window queue        the feed, off main    plain values       main: draws only the model
                                                ^                                        |
                MMWindowController <------------+------------- MMWindowHost: presses ----+
```

- **The view model**, `Sources/UI/Window/MMWindowModel.h`, is plain values with no AppKit and no Core type. Every string in it is shown as is.
- **The feed**, `Sources/Shell/MMWindowFeed.h`, makes one model from one snapshot and its `MMPerformance`, off main. `minmacs window --json` prints what it makes.
- **The controller**, `Sources/Shell/MMWindowController.h`, works only while the window is open. It builds one model per snapshot, applies a press that comes through `MMWindowHost`, and sends a new model. Fix Everything runs through `MMFixRunner`, which acts only through `MMRunAction` and `MMRunWrapUp`.
- **The log**, `Sources/Shell/MMWindowLog.h`, makes the middle's entries from the rows of `minmacs.sqlite`, off main. The controller reads it on its queue while the window is open, and again only when `MMLogStamp` moved. `minmacs log` prints the same entries.
- **The views**, `Sources/UI/Window`, run on main and read only the model: no disk, no process, no graph.
- **The holder**, `MMWindowHolder` in `Sources/UI/Window/MMWindowHolder.m`, owns the panel. Drawn once, the panel, its views and their layers hold tens of MB, so once the window has stayed closed for 45 seconds the panel is let go, and never while a run of Fix Everything plays. A quick reopen keeps the same panel. The next open makes it again from the last model, and its first frame is drawn and committed before it comes on screen, as at every open.

### Checked, not assumed

`tools/render.sh` draws the window from fixture models into `build/render/window/`, in dark and in dark with Increase Contrast: the main view, a pointed row, a row under the pointer with its white wash and an active row with another row under the pointer, `busy-hover-*` and `busy-active-*`, Settings, a run and its end, nothing to fix, one gain alone, the worktrees of five projects with too little room, `busy-projects-*`, a Caches group of 169 temp-folder families and four sessions' scratch, each kin one row, and its story, `caches-*`, a worktree's story with its pull request open, merged, none and still looked for, `busy-pr-*`, an agent's story with every fact, Go to and its presses, and one with almost none, `agents-*`, the welcome, and the log empty, on a new Mac and busy with 300 entries, scrolled and at its end, and with Undo presses and runs. It runs its checks of this page into `checks.txt`, and writes how long a first frame took to `window/open-ms.txt`. After a change to the window, look at the PNGs.

## The menus of before

With `defaults write no.guerrilla.minmacs core.window -bool NO`, and MinMacs opened again, each expert has its own icon and menu, or all five share one in "One Icon" mode, and Settings is a window of its own. The rules from here to "Words" are for those menus. They started in LocoHost and WorkForest, and research track G checked them against Apple's Human Interface Guidelines ([G-menu-ux.md](2.0/research/G-menu-ux.md)).

### The menu is native

The menu is an NSMenu. A popover was tried and felt slow next to the system's own menus. Custom views are used only for text meant to be read: the header, the summary and the hover card. Text that should be read is never a disabled item, because macOS greys those out. Section titles use `+[NSMenuItem sectionHeaderWithTitle:]`. A sentence that explains a mode or a setting goes in a hover card or the Settings window.

### The menu bar

1. **The icon.** Each expert's icon is a square template symbol at one point size, with no text. A number shows beside it only when it asks the person to act, and then at most three characters.
2. **Saved position.** Each status item sets `autosaveName` to `minmacs.<expert>` and allows removal. Settings shows the same visible or hidden state.
3. **Never rely on the icon.** The system hides icons that do not fit, and still reports them visible. Opening MinMacs again opens the Settings window on its overview pane. The CLI and notifications carry the rest.
4. **"One Icon" mode is flat.** The MinMacs menu holds one section per visible expert, with its header line and top rows. Experts never nest as submenus, so a card is never two levels deep.
5. **Accessibility label.** Each status item's label names the expert and its state, such as "LocoHost, 2 idle servers", never a symbol name.

### Every line earns its place

1. Every line answers a question the person has. Nothing decorative: no SF Symbols on items, no bars that need a legend, no unlabeled icon rows.
2. A number appears once. The header counts. Section headers do not repeat counts, and rows do not repeat what their section says.
3. The main menu holds at most:
   - a header of two lines
   - a summary of at most three lines, cut at a whole sentence and never mid-word
   - two actions
   - four sections of at most six rows each, with the rest under a "More" item
   - a footer of exactly three items: Hide Icon, Settings… and Quit MinMacs. Recently Stopped and its peers move into the expert's More item

   **When an action can't run.**
   - An action that cannot run right now stays in place, dimmed, with the reason in a readable gray tail, as the HIG advises. For example: "Remove…   3 uncommitted files".
   - An action that never applies here is hidden, such as Close Idle with nothing idle.
   - An Option alternate of an action does not count against the limit, and nothing is reachable only through Option.
4. A row is a name and at most three facts, ordered owner · size · state. No badge when the section already says it. A row's state mark differs in shape as well as color. Every expert uses the kit's one width.
5. A hover card holds:
   - a title of one or two lines
   - a colored status of at most two lines. It starts with a word, such as "Stays" or "Goes at 18:00", and says in plain words why the item stays or when it goes, and what happens next
   - at most five aligned facts, leaving out the empty ones. A fact that belongs to another expert links there, such as "Worktree   speed-3-base, merged", and counts as one of the five
   - the model's note in at most two lines, and only when it adds to the status
   - at most four actions, with the rest under More

## Words

These hold for the window, the menus, the CLI and notifications.

- **Plain words.** "Squash-merged", not "merged (squash)". "Remote branch deleted", not "upstream gone". "1 commit not in main", not "1 not in main".
- **Say where a removed thing goes.** Everything MinMacs removes keeps a way back, so the words say where: "Move to Trash" and "Undo moves it back" when it goes to the Trash, "A recovery copy is made first" for a tool's own cleanup. Only a record an older MinMacs wrote under its Delete choice says "cannot be brought back". Never promise an undo for a deleted thing. "For good" and "permanently" are not used.
- **No parentheses** as asides.
- **Gray tails** carry only time, size, or why an action can't run: "Review Now   9m ago", "Close Idle   2 servers, 1.2 GB".
- **Truncate** at the tail, never in the middle.
- **Capitalization.** Menu items use title-style capitalization and no articles. An item that opens more input ends in an ellipsis. Summaries, cards and notification bodies use sentence case.
- **Sizes.** A size is what deleting frees. When du says more, show du's figure only beside the freed figure, such as "2.8 MB freed, 1.5 GB on disk".
- **Energy** is a rank, such as "uses the most energy". Never show joules or watts.
- **Owned items.** A protected or owned item says who owns it and where its setting lives, such as "Claude Code keeps transcripts for 30 days".

## First launch

With the menu window, the first open shows the welcome, as above. With the menus of before there is no window and no wizard, and the login item is added at the first launch, with a line in the menu for a day that says so. On the first day each expert's summary says what it watches and when it will first act, such as "Watching 6 servers. Acts on nothing until tomorrow." An expert asks for notification permission the first time it acts on its own, never at launch. An expert whose field is empty on this Mac starts with its icon hidden, and shows it once, when the field appears.

## Notifications

A notification is sent only after an expert acted without being asked, one per review across all experts.
- **The title** is short, in title case, with no app name and no final period, such as "Stopped 2 Servers, Freed 1.2 GB".
- **The body** is full sentences in sentence case. It never sends the person somewhere in the menu.
- **One Undo action,** and the passive interruption level unless the disk is nearly full.
- **An error** is never an alert, D3: these Macs run with nobody at them. It is a notice, one plain sentence in the log, "Could not restore. The record is gone.", and at most one notification of it, inside the same limit of one per review, with no button.

## Nothing waits for a person

MinMacs shows no modal alert. A confirm guards only a press a person made in the window or a menu while plainly there, such as Remove on a worktree's card or lid mode turned on. It is never shown for the command line, an agent, the migration or anything an expert starts on its own, and never while the Mac runs unattended. A link can come from any web page, so a link that stops, removes or cleans, such as `workforest://clean-up`, `locohost://close-idle` or `run-now`, shows the same confirm while a person is at the Mac, and acts unasked only while it runs unattended. Then an action that keeps a way back runs as `--yes` would, and one that does not, such as an Investigate that costs tokens, does not run and a notice says how to run it. The one prompt left is macOS's own: the administrator password for the lid rule, the first time lid mode is turned on, and only on that explicit turn with a person here. On a Mac that runs unattended, lid mode turned on without the rule is refused at once with the notice "Lid mode needs a one-time setup", and `minmacs device --json` names it in `provision.needsPerson`.

## The menus, checked

After any change to a menu, run `tools/render.sh`. It draws every expert's status item, header, menu and hover cards, the shell's One Icon menu and the Settings panes from the fixture subjects in `tools/render.m`, with the same kit the app uses, to PNG in `build/render/`, in light, dark and Increase Contrast. It also runs its own checks of this standard, writes them to `checks.txt`, and exits 1 when one fails. CI keeps the PNGs. The README's pictures in `docs/img/` are copies of some of them, and `./test.sh docs` reports when they differ from a new render. Look at the PNGs and check that:

- titles and the first fact on each line are not cut off
- the fact columns line up from card to card
- spacing stays even
- nothing says the same thing twice
- each status item's accessibility label is not a symbol name
- the menu opens from the keyboard with Control-F8
- a card's status starts with a word, and its state mark has a shape
